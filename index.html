Model basis & assumptions

Hydrostatic at zone:HP = 0.052 × MW × (Zone depth − Fluid level depth), only the wet column counts.

Influx: influx height h = V / (ID² / 1029.4) bbl/ft. Lost head = (0.052·MW − gas grad) × h. SICP = max(0, PP − HPeff), and it is only possible if the zone is open to the wellbore (perforated and not isolated by a plug).

Kill weight:KMW = PPEMW + margin, rounded up to 0.1 ppg. Bullheaded through the kill line; the hole is assumed to be left full.

MASP (PCE):max(current WHP, PP − gas gradient × zone depth), i.e. a gas-to-surface case. High test = MASP + margin, checked against the PCE rated working pressure.

Surge/swab (Burkhardt): closed-end tool. va = vp·d²/(D²−d²), veff = va + Kc·vp. Laminar Bingham slot-flow gradient dp/dL = PV·v/(1000·(D−d)²) + YP/(200·(D−d)) [psi/ft; cP, ft/s, in, lbf/100ft²]. Reynolds number NRe = 757·MW·v·(D−d)/μa with μa = PV + 5·YP·(D−d)/v. Above 2100 the turbulent form dp/dL = f·MW·v²/(21.1·0.816·(D−d)) with f = 0.0791/NRe0.25 is used. Δp is integrated over the tool-string length only, segment by segment. Surge and swab have the same magnitude at the same speed; the sign differs.

Cable annulus ignored: for cable d/D < 0.1 the displaced flow is negligible, and the slot approximation is not valid there. A yield-point term applied along thousands of feet of cable would over-predict Δp. The slot approximation is also weaker for tool d/D < 0.3, so that case is flagged.

Not modeled: fluid inertia/acceleration surges, gel-strength breakover, temperature/pressure effects on rheology, and effects of the tool position relative to the zone.

Surface tension:T = wc(Ldry + BF·Lwet) + Wtool·BFtool − Plub·(π/4)·dcable² ± Ffriction, with BF = 1 − MW/65.5 (steel). + friction on POOH, − on RIH. Pump-out: the tool must outweigh P·Acable + friction to enter the well. Sheave friction and cable stretch are not modeled.

Bailer cap:cap ft = gal / (ID²/24.51).

References to verify against your copies: Burkhardt, J.A. (1961), "Wellbore Pressure Surges Produced by Pipe Movement," JPT; Bourgoyne et al., Applied Drilling Engineering, SPE Textbook Vol. 2, Ch. 4 (annular flow, slot approximation, surge). These are paraphrased here, not quoted.

Rules that come from your SOP/program, not physics: pack-off allowed only for logging/CBL on a dead well with no surface pressure; perforating always fully lubricated; correlation required before firing/setting/dumping; the length, test, tension and kill margins are inputs.
